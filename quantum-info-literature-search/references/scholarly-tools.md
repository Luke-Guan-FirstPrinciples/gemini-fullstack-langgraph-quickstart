# Scholarly Tools

Use this reference when the user wants explicit tool guidance for literature search.

## OpenAlex
Use OpenAlex for structured graph exploration.

Best uses:
- trace works, authors, institutions, concepts, and venues,
- follow related entities from a seed paper,
- map who collaborates with whom,
- compare venue or institutional patterns,
- inspect citation-network structure at a high level.

In this workflow, OpenAlex is strongest for:
- author expansion,
- lab or institution expansion,
- venue expansion,
- building a reproducible map of the literature.

## Google Scholar
Use Google Scholar for broad recall and quick citation navigation.

Best uses:
- broad first-pass retrieval,
- forward citation browsing,
- rough popularity checks,
- finding accessible versions of papers,
- checking whether a paper is broadly known.

Cautions:
- citation counts can overstate importance in trendy areas,
- metadata can be messy,
- ranking can mix central papers with merely well-matched keywords.

## Semantic Scholar
Use Semantic Scholar for fast paper overviews and related-paper discovery.

Best uses:
- quickly inspecting abstracts and paper summaries,
- tracing related papers,
- author and topic browsing,
- screening recent papers.

In this workflow, Semantic Scholar is especially useful during title/abstract triage and during expansion after reading a strong seed paper.

## X and social channels
Treat x and similar channels as weak signals.

Good uses:
- noticing what recent preprints are circulating,
- spotting informal critiques or enthusiasm,
- deciding which new papers deserve immediate inspection.

Bad uses:
- treating attention as evidence,
- substituting online discussion for technical reading,
- using virality as a proxy for correctness.

## Recommended division of labor
- Start broad with Google Scholar or Semantic Scholar.
- Use OpenAlex to map entities and relationships once a few seeds are known.
- Use Semantic Scholar or Google Scholar for fast screening and forward citation checking.
- Use x only to prioritize which recent papers may need immediate review.
